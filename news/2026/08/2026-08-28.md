## AI News Brief
**Friday, 28 August** · New creative models, agent controls and two market-shaping developments.

## 🧠 Models & Platforms
**Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash adds controllable video generation**

Google has released Omni 1.1 Flash through the Gemini API and AI Studio, adding scene extension, first-and-last-frame control, cheaper 360p drafts and up to 4K output. Creative teams gain a more production-shaped workflow, though speed and quality claims remain vendor-reported.

[Direct source ↗](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/build-with-gemini-omni-1-1-flash/)

**Gemini 3.5 Transcribe enters public preview**

Google’s new speech-to-text model now supports live and recorded audio through separate APIs, with custom vocabulary, timestamps, speaker attribution and more than 85 languages. Voice-app builders can test it now; Google’s accuracy and latency comparisons should still be validated on real audio.

[Direct source ↗](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-3-5-transcribe/)

## 🛠 Agents, Tools & Open Source
**Anthropic previews a standard for agents operating physical hardware**

The Model Hardware Standard connects agents to programmable lab and manufacturing equipment through common drivers, MCP, command-line tools and APIs. Access is limited to a research preview and the standard is not yet open source, so treat it as an early interoperability and safety test.

[Direct source ↗](https://www.anthropic.com/news/model-hardware-standard-research-preview)

**GitHub Copilot code review expands to bot and very large pull requests**

Copilot can now give full reviews to bot-authored pull requests—including Copilot agent work—and GitHub has removed its previous 300-file or 20,000-line review limit. Teams gain wider automated coverage, but should check the policy and billing path for reviews attributed directly to the organisation.

[Direct source ↗](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-08-27-copilot-code-review-resolution-reasons-and-expanded-capabilities/)

**GitHub starts enforcing its global Copilot model policy**

GitHub is rolling out default-policy enforcement through 1 September: unconfigured generally available models inherit the enterprise setting, while open-weight and data-retaining models remain disabled by default. Copilot administrators should review the default before model access changes automatically.

[Direct source ↗](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-08-26-global-model-policy-generally-available/)

## ⚖️ Industry, Policy & Trust
**Reported $12.9 billion Nvidia–Hugging Face deal is not final**

The Information reports that Nvidia is moving to acquire Hugging Face, with CNBC separately confirming talks, but neither company has announced a completed transaction. A deal could give Nvidia substantial influence over the leading open-model hub; users should monitor governance and hosting terms rather than assume any immediate change.

[Direct source ↗](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/08/report-nvidia-to-acquire-ai-model-repository-hugging-face-for-13-billion/)

**US judge blocks Anthropic’s Pentagon blacklist**

A federal judge permanently barred enforcement of measures that sought to cut Anthropic off from federal work, ruling the supply-chain-risk designation unlawful retaliation. The order does not require agencies to buy Claude, but it removes a sweeping exclusion that could have reshaped government and defence access.

[Direct source ↗](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/judge-rules-trump-administration-illegally-punished-ai-firm-anthropic/)
