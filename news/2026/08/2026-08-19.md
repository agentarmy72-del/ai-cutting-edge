## AI News Brief
**Wednesday, 19 August** · Safety gates, teen defaults, ads, agent payments and transparency rules move into real products.

## 🧠 Models & Platforms
**ChatGPT ads expand to 31 European markets**

OpenAI says ChatGPT Ads is expanding to 31 European markets. European users should review the consent and ad settings presented during rollout, while advertisers gain a new conversational channel.

[Direct source ↗](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-ads-expands-across-europe/)

**ChatGPT now has a default teen experience**

Accounts declared or estimated to be 13–17 now default into ChatGPT for Teens, with age-appropriate content limits, Study Hours, break reminders and parent controls. This gives families a safer starting configuration, but OpenAI’s safeguards are not a guarantee of safety.

[Direct source ↗](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-for-teens/)

**Databricks adds Precision Mode for complex document extraction**

Databricks says its new AI Extract Precision Mode pairs custom extraction models with an agentic harness for long documents and complex schemas. Its claimed seven-point accuracy lead over a GPT-5.6 Sol baseline is a vendor benchmark, so teams should test it on their own documents.

[Direct source ↗](https://www.databricks.com/blog/databricks-document-intelligence-pushing-frontier-complex-document-extraction)

## 🛠 Agents, Tools & Open Source
**AWS makes AgentCore payments generally available**

AWS says Bedrock AgentCore payments can let agents pay for APIs, MCP services and content, with wallet integrations, spending guardrails and observability. Builders can now test paid-agent workflows without inventing the payment layer, but should keep budgets and approvals explicit.

[Direct source ↗](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/amazon-bedrock-agentcore-payments-is-now-generally-available-enabling-agents-to-transact-safely-and-autonomously-at-scale/)

## ⚖️ Industry, Policy & Trust
**OpenAI keeps its largest frontier RL run on hold**

OpenAI says it paused reinforcement-learning training on its latest deployment models for two weeks and its largest planned frontier run remains on hold, after the OpenAI–Hugging Face security incident and preliminary evidence that Astra may meet its Critical cyber threshold. It has raised workload-isolation and monitoring requirements, signalling that frontier release timing is now explicitly safety-gated.

[Direct source ↗](https://openai.com/index/pacing-model-development-cyber-capabilities/)

**Japan publishes a revised AI transparency-code draft**

The nonbinding “comply or explain” draft asks public-facing developers and providers—including overseas services offered in Japan—to disclose model lineage, training methods, dataset categories and crawler details, or explain omissions. Providers with Japanese users should audit their documentation before the code is finalised.

[Direct source ↗](https://www.cas.go.jp/jp/seisakukaigi/titeki2/ai_kentoukai/gijisidai/dai13/index.html)

**Diffusion-model study finds individual training sources can become unattributable**

A Nature Communications study reports that, under its tested conditions, diffusion models trained on enough data could often omit any single sample or creator without materially changing a generated output. That complicates post-hoc training-data attribution; the finding is specific to diffusion models and should not be generalised to every AI system.

[Direct source ↗](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75667-5)
